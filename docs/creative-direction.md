## A. Storyboard analysis

Your sketch already contains the whole film: a serpentine path that zig-zags down the page, carrying one shirt through five states and into a machine and back out. That zig-zag is the strongest idea on the page, because it naturally creates the left/right rhythm for typography.

### What the sketch communicates (Observed)

| Frame | Drawn | Note on page | Path direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Shirt laid flat, sleeves out, buttons visible, top-left | "Dirty text", with "DIRTY" underlined | Arc sweeps right and down |
| 2 | Same shirt, tilted, one sleeve lifted, right side | "Dirty shirt" (label sits on the left) | Long arc sweeps back left |
| 3 | Shirt compressed and spinning, motion lines under it, left side | "Round" | Short arc pushes right |
| 4 | Front-loader with a round porthole; shirt visible inside the drum | "Cleaning" (label on the left) | Two lines exit the porthole, curving left |
| 5 | Shirt flung out mid-air, tilted, right of centre | "Out shirt" | Drops into final frame |
| 6 | Shirt upright, flat, centred-left, calm | "Fresh and clean shirt" | Rest |
| Header | "UI" with a tick; #72E5F2 and #062E3D | — | — |

### Strongest ideas

- **The zig-zag path.** Each frame lands on the opposite side from the last, which gives you free alternating text columns.
- **The "Round" frame before the machine.** You drew the spin starting *outside* the drum. That's a great anticipation beat: the shirt begins to tumble before it is swallowed, so the machine feels like a continuation of motion, not a new scene.
- **The porthole as a lens.** The shirt drawn inside the circular window means the drum can double as a frame-within-the-canvas without breaking the "no cards" rule, because it is a physical object, not a UI box.
- **The exit as a fling.** "Out shirt" is drawn mid-tumble, not neatly placed. That energy release before the calm final pose is good pacing.
- **The two hex codes.** Light aqua for atmosphere and deep teal for ink is already the right pairing.

### Unclear or technically hard transitions (Recommended fixes)

1. **"Dirty text" (frame 1).** Ambiguous: either the headline itself is dirty/smudged, or text appears describing dirt. Recommendation: the hero word **"DIRTY"** is set in deep teal with a subtle smudged/grimy texture, which washes clean later. It becomes a typographic echo of the shirt.
2. **Frame 2 vs frame 1.** The sketch shows the shirt in two places but the change between them is mostly position. Give this beat a job: stains *reveal* here (a raking-light pass shows what the clean-looking shirt was hiding).
3. **Entering the drum (3 → 4).** The hardest shot. A shirt flying into a porthole looks fake if the door just swallows it. Fix: the camera pushes in so the porthole grows to fill the viewport, the shirt passes through the glass plane, and we're now *inside* the drum. No hard cut.
4. **Inside the drum (4).** Water, foam and fabric simulation in real time is expensive on the web. Solve it with pre-rendered image sequences or video for the drum interior, scrubbed by scroll, with only the shirt's stain-fade and the typography being live.
5. **Exit (4 → 5).** Your two curved exit lines suggest the shirt spirals out. Reverse the entry: pull back out of the porthole, door swings open, shirt tumbles out in the mirror of how it entered.
6. **Rest pose (6).** The final shirt is drawn flat-front and centred. Recommend it hangs from an implied hanger point (no visible hanger, or a single fine line) so it settles with a gentle pendulum, which reads as "pressed and ready" rather than "laid on the floor".
7. **Missing beat: ironing.** Ironing/pressing is one of your services but has no frame. Add a short "Press" beat between the fling and the rest pose: the tumbling shirt flattens in mid-air as if a sheet of steam passes through it.

### Principles I'd look to extract (Recommended, from the genre)

- **Hero object as protagonist.** Premium product sites (headphones, sneakers, watches) keep one cut-out object on a seamless background and let scroll drive its pose. This is exactly your shirt.
- **One light source for the whole page.** The illusion of one canvas depends on shadows always falling the same way, even as the object moves.
- **Oversized type behind the object.** A huge word set behind the subject creates depth with no boxes. The object overlaps the letters.
- **Pinned stage, moving copy.** The visual stays pinned while text blocks scroll past it, so reading never fights the animation.
- **Scrub, don't trigger.** Motion maps 1:1 to scroll position, so the visitor feels in control, rather than animations firing and finishing on their own.

### Typical traps to avoid copying

- Glossy 3D renders with plastic-looking materials (fabric must feel soft and matte).
- Neon gradients and glass panels common in tech landing pages.
- Parallax for its own sake, where every layer moves at a different speed and nothing feels anchored.
- Bento-grid service sections that break the single-canvas promise.

## C. Creative concept: The Clean Thread

**Your scroll is the wash cycle.** The visitor doesn't read about laundry; they run one load from start to finish with their thumb or trackpad. The shirt is the protagonist, the pale aqua air is the world it lives in, and a single invisible thread, the path from your sketch, ties every scene together.

The page opens on silence: a white shirt hanging in mint-pale air, slightly greyed, a coffee mark on the placket, a scuff at the collar. A huge word sits behind it, **DIRTY**, set in deep teal with a grimy texture. Nothing is boxed. As the visitor scrolls, the shirt turns towards an unseen light and we see everything it was hiding. It drifts down the thread, starts to tumble, and is drawn into the glass of a washing machine that rises out of the same air it has been floating in.

Inside, the world briefly changes temperature: deeper teal, light refracting through water, foam curling around the fabric. Each stain dissolves in step with the scroll, and each one that disappears names the service that removed it. Then the door opens, the shirt is released, steam passes through it like a pressing iron, and it settles, crisp and bright, on an invisible hanger. The word behind it has quietly changed from **DIRTY** to **FRESH**. One button: **Book a Pickup**.

The idea is deliberately simple to say in one sentence, so every design decision can be checked against it: *does this keep it one shirt, one canvas, one continuous move?* If not, it goes.

**Brand line options (Recommended):** "Every thread, cared for." · "From worn to wow, one load at a time." · "We'll take it from here."

## D. Visual direction

Quiet, airy, tactile: think a high-end fabric atelier photographed in soft daylight, not a tech product launch. The shirt and the type do the work; colour is used like a highlighter, never a paint roller.

### Colour hierarchy

| Role | Colour | Share of screen | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atmosphere (background) | Very pale aqua, derived: `#F2FBFB` to `#E6F8F7` | \~80% | The whole canvas. A barely-there radial lift behind the shirt, no visible gradient bands |
| Ink | Deep teal `#062E3D` | \~10% | Headlines, body copy, nav, the drum interior's deepest shade, footer |
| Light & water | Light aqua `#72E5F2` | \~4% | Water highlights, glass reflections, hover underlines |
| Cleanliness signal | Mint `#69F9B7` | \~2% | Only at the moment a stain disappears (a soft sparkle), the CTA's hover state, "done" ticks |
| Process accent | Teal `#5CDBC5` | \~2% | Scene numbers, small labels, progress thread |
| Highlight | Accent blue `#699FF9` | <1% | Links and focus states only |
| Paper | White `#FFFFFF` | \~2% | The clean shirt itself; the CTA text |

Rule of thumb: if a bright colour fills more than a word or a thin line, it's too much.

### Background

One continuous pale aqua field for the whole story. No section dividers, no colour blocks. Depth comes from a soft, out-of-focus floor shadow under the shirt and a subtle paper-grain/noise layer (2–3% opacity) so it reads as a physical space rather than a flat hex. The only time the environment changes is inside the drum, where the canvas deepens towards deep teal, and it returns to pale aqua on exit.

### Typography

- **Display:** a high-contrast editorial serif (e.g. Fraunces, Canela, or GT Super) for single-word headlines like DIRTY / FRESH, set very large (18–24vw) behind the shirt.
- **Text & UI:** a clean neo-grotesk (e.g. Inter Tight, Neue Montreal, or Satoshi) for service names, body copy, and buttons.
- **Hierarchy:** giant word (background layer) → scene headline (48–64px serif) → service name (14px grotesk, uppercase, tracked, teal) → 2-line body (17–18px) → a single text link.
- Copy blocks are narrow (max 32 characters a line for headlines, 52 for body) and sit in the empty side opposite the shirt.

### The shirt

- A crisp white cotton poplin or oxford button-down: universally readable, shows dirt clearly, and white is the best canvas for the reveal.
- Matte fabric, visible weave at close range, natural soft folds at the elbows and waist, slightly rumpled collar in the dirty state, sharp collar points in the clean state.
- Realistic, believable stains: a coffee drip on the placket, a grey ring at the collar, a grass/soil scuff on one cuff, and a faint ink mark near the pocket. Four stains, four services.
- One shirt asset throughout. Stains live on a separate layer so they can fade independently.

### The washing machine

- A real-feeling, minimal front-loader in warm white with a brushed chrome porthole ring. No logos, no cartoon dials; one subtle control knob and a thin display.
- It never appears as a product shot. It *rises into* the canvas from below, cropped, so we mainly see the porthole. Its base shadow matches the shirt's.
- Inside the drum: real water caustics, slow foam, the stainless-steel drum perforations catching light.

### Lighting & shadows

- One key light from the upper left, about 45°, soft and large (an overcast north window). Shadows always fall down-right.
- A soft contact shadow on an invisible floor about 120px below the shirt. It stretches and fades as the shirt rises and shrinks as it nears.
- Clean state: slightly brighter, cooler white balance, as if the shirt is now returning more light.

### Art direction rules

1. Nothing ever sits in a box. If it needs a container, it doesn't belong on the canvas.
2. Every scene has one big object, one big word, one small text block.
3. Negative space is at least 50% of every frame.
4. Motion always follows the thread: no element enters from an unrelated direction.

## E. Scroll storyboard

Your five beats become eight scenes across one pinned stage roughly 900vh tall (about 9 screen-heights of scroll on desktop). Scroll percentages refer to that stage. Positions are given as a share of the viewport; scale 1.0 = the shirt fills about 45% of viewport height.

| # | Scene | Scroll | Shirt side | Service introduced | Giant word |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Suspended | 0–8% | Centre-right | — (brand intro) | DIRTY |
| 02 | The Reveal | 8–20% | Right → left | Pickup & Delivery | DIRTY |
| 03 | The Tumble | 20–30% | Left → centre | — (anticipation) | — |
| 04 | Into the Glass | 30–40% | Centre (porthole) | Wash & Fold | — |
| 05 | The Wash | 40–65% | Centre, in drum | Stain Treatment, Delicate Care, Dry Cleaning | CLEAN (forming) |
| 06 | Release | 65–76% | Centre → right | — | — |
| 07 | The Press | 76–86% | Right → centre | Ironing & Pressing | — |
| 08 | Fresh | 86–100% | Centre-left, at rest | Book a Pickup (CTA) | FRESH |

### 01 — Suspended (0–8%)

- **Shirt:** centre-right (x 58%, y 48%), scale 1.0, front-facing with a 6° tilt. An idle loop before any scroll: a slow 6-second breathing float (±8px vertical, ±1.5° sway), hem rippling faintly as if in a draught.
- **Stains:** present but subtle at 35% opacity; the shirt just looks a bit tired and greyed.
- **Machine:** absent.
- **Type:** "DIRTY" in giant serif behind the shirt, offset left so the shirt overlaps its last two letters, with a grimy texture. Left column, small: brand name + "Premium laundry, picked up and returned." A small "Scroll to start the cycle" cue with a thin vertical line, the beginning of the thread.
- **Transition:** first scroll ticks ease the idle float into scroll-driven motion so there's no jump.

### 02 — The Reveal (8–20%)

- **Shirt:** travels along a wide arc from right to left (x 58% → 34%), rotating towards the light (Y-rotation 0° → -25°), with the sleeve on the far side lifting, matching your second sketch frame.
- **Stains:** a raking light sweeps across the shirt; as it passes, stains snap up to 100% opacity, one after another: collar ring, coffee drip, ink mark, cuff scuff. Each gets a fine teal hairline callout (no boxes) with a one-word label: Collar. Coffee. Ink. Grass.
- **Type:** right column. Headline: "Life happens to your clothes." Body: "Coffee on the commute, grass at the weekend match. We collect it all from your door." Service tag: **PICKUP & DELIVERY**.
- **Transition:** "DIRTY" drifts up and fades out; the shirt's arc continues downwards, picking up speed.

### 03 — The Tumble (20–30%)

- **Shirt:** your "Round" frame. It loosens and starts to rotate on its Z axis (0° → 160°) while compressing slightly (scale 1.0 → 0.8) and sleeves fold inwards. Motion streaks are replaced by realistic soft motion blur and a few droplets (the first hint of water).
- **Stains:** fully visible.
- **Machine:** begins to rise into frame from the bottom-centre, cropped, porthole first. Its contact shadow appears on the same invisible floor as the shirt's.
- **Type:** none. A deliberate breath; let the motion carry it.
- **Transition:** the shirt's path bends down towards the porthole centre.

### 04 — Into the Glass (30–40%)

- **Shirt:** reaches the porthole centre, scale 0.8 → 0.55 as the door glass catches its reflection.
- **Machine:** the camera pushes in. The porthole grows from 40% to 140% of viewport width, so its rim passes off-screen and we're now looking *through* the glass. The door seal and chrome ring frame the edges for a beat, then exit the viewport.
- **Environment:** background deepens from pale aqua to deep teal as we cross the glass plane.
- **Type:** left side, now in white: "In it goes." Service tag: **WASH & FOLD**. Body: "Sorted by colour and fabric, washed at the right temperature, folded by hand."
- **Transition:** the glass plane passes the camera with a soft refraction ripple.

### 05 — The Wash (40–65%), the longest scene

- **Shirt:** centred, scale 0.9, tumbling slowly as the drum rotates (the drum does most of the moving; the shirt rotates at about a third of its speed so fabric reads clearly). Foam and water swirl around it; caustic light ripples across the fabric.
- **Stains:** fade one by one, each in sync with a service line. The moment a stain fully vanishes, a tiny mint glint marks the spot.
  - 40–48%: coffee drip fades → **STAIN TREATMENT** (right): "Every mark pre-treated by hand before it ever meets water."
  - 48–56%: collar ring fades, plus a silk scarf briefly tumbles past in the background → **DELICATE FABRIC CARE** (left): "Silks, wools and linens get their own gentle cycle."
  - 56–65%: ink and cuff marks fade → **DRY CLEANING** (right): "For the pieces water can't touch, solvent-free professional dry cleaning."
- **Giant word:** "CLEAN" slowly assembles from bubbles behind the shirt, at low contrast.
- **Note:** dry cleaning isn't literally a wet wash; the copy frames it as part of the same care rather than claiming the drum does it. An alternative is to let the drum's water drain into a mist for this beat.
- **Transition:** drum slows, water drains, light brightens.

### 06 — Release (65–76%)

- **Shirt:** reverse of scene 04. The camera pulls back out through the glass, the door swings open, and the shirt is flung out along your two curved exit lines, arcing up and to the right (x 50% → 66%), tumbling once, scale 0.9 → 1.0. Fine water droplets shake off and catch the light.
- **Environment:** deep teal returns to pale aqua.
- **Machine:** sinks back down out of frame along the path it came.
- **Stains:** none. The fabric is visibly brighter.
- **Type:** none. Another breath.

### 07 — The Press (76–86%)

- **Shirt:** still damp and wrinkled; it slows at the top of its arc. A horizontal band of soft steam travels down through it, and the wrinkles flatten as it passes, like a pressing iron made of air. Collar points sharpen, the placket straightens.
- **Type:** left column. Service tag: **IRONING & PRESSING**. Headline: "Pressed, not just dried." Body: "Steam-pressed, collars set, ready on a hanger."
- **Transition:** shirt drifts down to its rest position.

### 08 — Fresh (86–100%)

- **Shirt:** settles centre-left (x 40%) from an implied hanger point, landing with a tiny pendulum (3° → 0° over 1.2s). Scale 1.05: slightly bigger and brighter than scene 01. Idle loop returns: an even slower, calmer breathing float.
- **Type:** "FRESH" giant serif behind the shirt, clean, no texture, the shirt overlapping its first letters (the mirror of DIRTY). Right column: "Fresh and clean, back at your door in 48 hours." Primary CTA **Book a Pickup** (deep teal pill, white text, mint on hover). Secondary text link: "See pricing".
- **Transition:** the stage unpins; the page continues into the supporting sections below with the same background, so it never feels like you've left the canvas.

## F. Page structure

The film is the first two-thirds of the homepage; everything after it is calmer, static and quick to scan, on the same pale aqua canvas. The final clean shirt stays visible at reduced scale in the first section below, so the story hands off rather than ending.

1. **Header (fixed, minimal).** Wordmark left; "Services", "Pricing", "How it works" centre; **Book a Pickup** button right. Transparent over the canvas, deep teal text. On scroll inside the drum (scenes 04–06), text flips to white.
   - A thin teal progress thread down the right edge shows wash-cycle progress (scene numbers 01–08).
2. **The Clean Thread (scroll film, scenes 01–08).** Hero + all service storytelling, as in section E.
3. **How it works (process).** Four steps on one horizontal line, the thread continuing as a connecting rule, with no cards: Book (pick a slot) → We collect (free pickup) → We clean (48h) → We return (pressed, folded, on hangers). Small line icons drawn in the same stroke weight as the thread.
4. **Services and pricing at a glance.** An editorial list rather than a card grid: six rows, service name in serif left, one-line description centre, "from ₹\_\_\_" right, hairline dividers between rows. Each row links to its own service page.
5. **Proof (trust signals).** Large typographic stats (garments cleaned, average rating, on-time return rate) set like the giant words. Below, two or three short customer quotes in italic serif, each with a name and neighbourhood. Then a quiet strip of guarantees: "Damage protection", "Eco-friendly detergents", "48h turnaround". Use real figures only; leave placeholders until you have them.
6. **Fabric care detail (optional).** One close-up macro of the clean shirt's weave, full-bleed but without a frame, with a short paragraph about the care process. Builds the premium feel.
7. **Final CTA.** The clean shirt returns small, centred, gently floating. Headline "Your next load, handled." Postcode/area field + **Book a Pickup** button inline.
8. **Footer (deep teal).** The only full deep-teal block on the page, like the inside of the drum, bookending the experience. Service areas, hours, contact, WhatsApp link, social, legal.

**Mobile:** same story, shorter (about 600vh), shirt always centred, copy below rather than beside it, giant words reduced to 28vw and kept behind the shirt.

## G. Motion design plan

Build it as one pinned stage with one master timeline scrubbed by scroll. Every element reads from the same progress value (0–1), so nothing can drift out of sync and there are never separate section animations.

### Recommended production approach

| Element | How it's made | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shirt (all scenes) | Pre-rendered image sequence from a 3D cloth simulation (Blender / Marvelous Designer / Houdini), about 240–360 frames, transparent WebP/AVIF, drawn to a canvas | Real cloth physics and fabric texture that the browser can't simulate live; frame-scrubbing looks identical to video but is perfectly scroll-controlled |
| Stains | Separate image layer (or a mask in the render pass) per stain, opacity driven by the timeline | Lets each stain fade exactly with its service copy, and lets you retime without re-rendering |
| Washing machine | Same 3D scene, rendered as its own sequence (rise, push-in, pull-out) | Lighting and perspective match the shirt automatically |
| Drum interior (water, foam, caustics) | Short seamless video loops or a sequence composited behind the shirt | Fluid sim is too heavy live; a looped plate is cheap and convincing |
| Background colour shift | CSS/WebGL colour interpolation on the canvas | Lightweight, smooth |
| Giant words, copy, CTA | Real HTML text, animated with transforms and opacity | Accessible, selectable, SEO-friendly, crisp at any size |
| Thread, callout lines, progress | SVG paths with animated stroke | Sharp and tiny |

Typical stack: GSAP ScrollTrigger (pin + scrub) or a Lenis smooth-scroll + custom timeline, with a canvas for the image sequences. A WebGL route (Three.js with a baked cloth animation) is the premium alternative if you want real-time lighting, at higher build cost.

### What moves and what stays still

- **Animated:** the shirt (position, rotation, scale, frame), stain layers, the machine, the drum interior, background tone, giant words, the progress thread.
- **Stable:** the header, the light direction, the floor plane and shadow angle, the type scale, the column grid, and the background texture. These constants are what make it feel like one world.

### Rules for smooth, intentional motion

1. **Scrub with smoothing.** Map scroll to the timeline with a short lag (about 0.6–1s of easing) so trackpad jitter turns into glide.
2. **One curve family.** Use the same ease (e.g. a gentle ease-in-out) for every travel move; reserve a slight overshoot only for the final hanger settle.
3. **Never two hero moves at once.** When the shirt travels, text holds still; when text enters, the shirt slows. Each scene has one dominant motion.
4. **Copy enters from the thread's side.** Text slides 24px in the same direction the shirt is moving and fades in over 10% of the scene; it never flies in from elsewhere.
5. **Breaths are deliberate.** Scenes 03 and 06 have no copy. They let motion be the content and stop the page feeling like a slideshow.
6. **Idle loops only at rest.** The float/breathing loop runs only in scenes 01 and 08 and blends in and out over half a second.

### Performance & accessibility

- Preload the first 30 frames for an instant hero; stream the rest progressively. Use smaller frame sets on mobile (about 720px wide).
- Target 60fps on a mid-range Android phone, since that's a large share of your likely audience.
- **Reduced motion:** honour `prefers-reduced-motion` by replacing the film with three still states (dirty, in the drum, fresh) cross-faded on scroll, with all copy intact.
- Keep all copy as real text in reading order so screen readers get the whole story even without the visuals.
- Book a Pickup must be reachable without scrolling the film: it lives in the header from the first frame.
